Installing OpenMote in a Wiimote shell

Swap the original Wii Remote board for an OpenMote. You will open the shell, move the B trigger over to a new bracket, and drop in a rechargeable battery. No soldering required.

Step by Step

Step1

Remove the Battery Cover

  • Remove the wrist strap and any silicone jacket from the Wii Remote.
  • Flip the remote over so the B trigger faces up.
  • Press the tab at the bottom of the battery cover and slide it off.
  • Take out both AA batteries. The OpenMote runs on its own rechargeable cell, so you won’t need them again.
Step2

Remove the Tri-Wing Screws

  • Remove the four Y1 tri-wing screws inside the battery bay, two near the top, two at the bottom.
  • Note: Keep the screws together. You’ll reuse all four in Step 12.
Step3

Release the Shell Clips

  • Insert an opening pick into the seam between the top and bottom shells, near the battery bay.
  • Push the pick straight down into the seam, pointing toward the bottom shell. Don’t hold it flat or at an angle.
  • Note: Pointing the pick down pushes the clips apart instead of prying against them, so they release without breaking.
  • Slide the pick along the seam toward the trigger end, in the direction of the arrow, to release the clips. Work down one side, then the other.
  • Caution: Go slowly around the trigger. The clips there are short and can snap if you twist the pick.
Step4

Separate the Shells

  • Lift the top shell straight up and set it face-down beside the bottom shell.
  • Caution: Keep the top shell’s button side facing the table as you lift it. If you flip it over, the loose buttons and membranes will fall out.
  • The original board stays seated in the bottom shell. The rubber button membranes and speaker stay in the top shell.
Step5

Lift Out the Original Board

  • Grip the board by its edges and lift it out of the bottom shell.
  • The B trigger assembly comes out attached to the underside of the board.
Step6

Check the Top Shell

  • Make sure every rubber button membrane is seated flat in the top shell.
  • Note: A membrane that has shifted will make its button feel mushy or not register at all.
Step7

Seat the OpenMote Board

  • Line up the OpenMote board with the top shell, USB-C port at the bottom end.
  • Lower the board in and press gently along its length until it sits flat on the membranes.
  • Alignment: Line up the six holes on the board with the posts in the shell, circled in yellow.
  • Note: The board should drop in without force. If it rocks, lift it out and re-check the membranes.
Step8

Fit the B Trigger Bracket

  • Take the white B trigger bracket from your kit.
  • Place it over the top end of the board, posts facing up, and press it down until it sits flat.
  • Alignment: Line up the two holes on the bracket with the two holes on the board, circled in yellow, before you press it down.
Step9

Install the B Button

  • Hold the B button with the rounded button face pointing up, away from the board.
  • Turn it so the half-moon flange points toward the ESP32 chip, away from the trigger end of the remote.
  • Drop it into the round opening on the bracket so it sits flush.
Step10

Insert the 3D Printed Port Cover

  • Take the 3D printed port cover and press it into the opening at the bottom end of the bottom shell.
  • The cover fills the gap around the USB-C port so the end of the remote looks clean.
  • Note: No printer? Download the STL and order it from any print service.
  • Download the port cover STL
Step11

Close the Shell

  • Lower the bottom shell onto the top shell, starting at the trigger end.
  • Check that the B button is seated on top of its membrane button before you close the shell.
  • Alignment: The reset button on the board fits through the round hole in the bottom shell, circled in yellow. Line them up as you close the shell.
  • Squeeze the top of the remote where the two clips are, one clip at a time, until each one snaps shut.
  • Caution: Don’t force it. If the seam won’t close, open the shell and check the bracket and B button are seated.
Step12

Reinstall the Screws

  • Reinstall the four tri-wing screws you removed in Step 2, in the locations circled.
  • Caution: Stop as soon as each screw is snug. Overtightening can strip the plastic posts.
Step13

Connect the Battery

Negative −Positive +
Negative −Positive +
  • Plug the battery connector into the socket at the bottom of the battery bay.
  • Negative (black wire) goes on top. Positive (red wire) goes on the bottom.
  • Caution: Don’t install the battery backwards. Match the wires to the Negative and Positive labels in the photo before you plug it in. Backwards will damage the board.
  • Tuck the wires to the side and set the battery into the bay.
  • Caution: Never puncture, bend or crush a lithium battery.
Step14

Replace the Battery Cover

  • Slide the battery cover back on until it clicks.
  • Your OpenMote is ready. Charge it over USB-C before first use.
Conclusion

Done. Your OpenMote is in.
