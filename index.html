Questions, Answered.

Straight answers for the smart home, the emulator rig, and the workbench. Updated August 2026.

What is OpenMote?

OpenMote is a programmable remote control built on the ESP32-S3: twelve buttons, a 6-axis motion sensor, infrared in and out, WiFi, and Bluetooth 5 LE, in a classic Wii-remote-style shell. It is made by Hat & Hammer, a two-person hardware studio in Los Angeles. The bare board is $59 and the fully assembled remote is $99 on Crowd Supply, launching soon.

Can I put OpenMote inside my old Wii remote?

Yes, that is the whole idea. The OpenMote board is a drop-in replacement for the board inside an original Wii remote, the one you may know as the Wiimote: open the shell, swap the board, close it up. No soldering, no adhesive, no drama.

So far it has dropped into every official Wii remote shell we have tried. Third-party shells usually copy the original closely, but they do vary inside, so the guaranteed fit is for Nintendo's own shells. And if you would rather not open anything, the fully assembled OpenMote remote comes ready to go in its own shell.

Does it work with an actual Wii console?

No, and we would rather you hear it from us. The original console talks to its remotes over Bluetooth Classic, and OpenMote is built on Bluetooth 5 LE, the modern standard that PCs, phones, and emulators use. OpenMote is for everything else: the emulator rig, the smart home, and whatever you build next.

Does OpenMote work with Home Assistant?

Yes. OpenMote works with Home Assistant out of the box, over your own network. Real physical buttons run your scenes and automations: lights, thermostats, media, anything Home Assistant can reach. And because you program the firmware, you decide what a single press, a double press, a long press, or a flick of the wrist actually does.

Does OpenMote need the cloud?

No. OpenMote connects over your own WiFi and Bluetooth. There is no account to create and no subscription. You flash the firmware yourself, so you always know exactly what the remote is doing and who it talks to.

Does it work with Alexa, Google Home, or Apple Home?

Yes, through Home Assistant. Its bridges connect OpenMote to Google Home, Amazon Alexa, and Apple Home, and its Matter integration lets OpenMote's buttons drive your Matter devices too. One remote can cover the whole living room: the TV over infrared, the lights over WiFi, the games over Bluetooth.

Can OpenMote replace my old universal remote?

That is one of its favorite jobs. OpenMote carries a 940nm infrared emitter and a 38kHz receiver, so it can learn codes straight from any remote you point at it, then be that remote: TV, receiver, cable box, projector. If you have been nursing a discontinued Harmony remote, this is the programmable replacement. WiFi and Bluetooth pick up everything infrared cannot reach.

Does OpenMote work with the Dolphin emulator?

Yes. OpenMote pairs as a standard Bluetooth gamepad on Windows, Mac, and Linux, with real motion data from its accelerometer and gyroscope. In Dolphin you map it as an emulated Wii Remote with motion input: no sensor bar adapter, no special Bluetooth stack, no sync-button ritual. The same goes for RetroArch and anything else that accepts a Bluetooth controller.

Can I use it as a game controller on my PC or phone?

Yes. OpenMote shows up as a real Bluetooth HID gamepad on a PC, Mac, or phone, the same way a modern controller does. Buttons and motion are yours to map in firmware, and the haptic motor answers back with rumble.

Can I program OpenMote with the Arduino IDE?

Yes. OpenMote programs like any ESP32: Arduino IDE, ESP-IDF, PlatformIO, or CircuitPython, all over USB-C. The OpenMote.h Arduino library covers the buttons, motion, sound, infrared, and LEDs. If you have ever flashed an ESP32 dev board, you already know how this works.

Is OpenMote open source?

It will be. Complete schematics, PCB layouts, the bill of materials, and the Arduino library go public on GitHub before units ship. The board is built around the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1, a pre-certified module the maker community already knows inside and out.

Do I need to solder anything?

No. The board drops into the shell of an original Wii remote with every connection already made: buttons, battery, speaker, all of it. For expansion there is a Qwiic / STEMMA QT port, so thousands of off-the-shelf I2C sensors plug straight in, also without soldering.

Can OpenMote be a MIDI controller?

Yes, with the right firmware. The ESP32-S3 can speak MIDI over Bluetooth LE, so OpenMote can appear to your computer as a wireless MIDI device. Map buttons to notes and the motion sensor to CC messages and you have an instrument that fits in one hand.
